# Kale's taste profile — extracted from Pinterest "Clothes" board

Source: **all 50** corpus images viewed in detail (36 on 2026-07-07, the remaining 14 on
2026-08-07; board has 111 pins total, 50 retrievable without auth). Confidence: HIGH — the
board is unusually coherent, and the second pass confirmed rather than revised the core read.
Votes in `taste/votes.json` override this document wherever they conflict.

## The one-line read
**Vintage-leaning relaxed streetwear built on earthy neutrals** — washed workwear and
chunky knits over baggy light denim or carpenter/cargo pants, finished with retro
sneakers and silver jewelry. Thrifted-feel over box-fresh; silhouette over logo.

## Silhouette (the strongest signal, ~90% of outfit pins)
- EVERYTHING relaxed-to-baggy: boxy cropped knits, oversized tees/sweatshirts, roomy
  overshirts. Not a single slim-fit outfit on the board.
- Pants are the anchor: baggy light-wash jeans (often raw/cut hem), carpenter/double-knee
  work pants (tan, brown, washed grey), khaki/cream cargos, occasional linen or
  sage trousers. Straight-to-wide leg, pooling or cuffed at the ankle.
- Layering formula: white tee or tank as base → open overshirt / chore jacket /
  flannel / zip hoodie → optional heavier third layer (varsity, field jacket).

## Palette
- Core: olive/sage, tan/khaki, cream, brown, washed grey, faded black, light-wash denim blue.
- Denim is always LIGHT wash or heavily faded — dark rigid denim appears once, ever.
- Accents allowed: one loud piece per outfit max (red patterned knit, varsity maroon,
  UNC-blue Dunks) against an otherwise muted fit.

## Recurring garments (ranked by frequency)
1. Baggy light-wash jeans (raw hem, cuffed, or distressed)
2. Carpenter / double-knee work pants in tan-brown-grey (Carhartt/Dickies energy, some
   paint-splatter Gallery Dept vibes)
3. Chore jackets & military overshirts (olive OG-107 style, M-65 field jacket, washed black)
4. Chunky vintage knits: boxy cropped crewnecks, striped grandpa knits, patterned
   cardigans, statement knits (incl. a Kapital-style bone-intarsia sleeve knit)
5. Oversized vintage tees/sweatshirts (band tees, Harley, thrift graphics, college print)
6. Cream/neutral tonal sets (Essentials-style knit + cream jeans) — the "clean" end
7. Varsity jackets (maroon leather-sleeve, green/cream)
8. Ribbed tanks + pearl/silver necklace (summer minimal-street)

## Footwear (very consistent)
- Retro Nike dominates: Air Force 1 (white, most common), Dunk Low (incl. UNC blue),
  Jordan 1 (highs and lows), Jordan 4 (white/neutral)
- Black Dr. Martens boots (grunge fits), tan western/leather boots (workwear fits)
- Suede clogs/mules (Birkenstock Boston style) for the relaxed-clean fits
- Converse (black + white hi/lo), cream Reebok Club C style, grey New Balance runners,
  chunky Yeezy 700-style on the tonal-streetwear end
- Signal: worn-in > pristine, gum soles and cream tones over bright white everywhere
  except AF1s

## Jewelry & accessories (near-constant)
- Silver: chunky rings (often multiple), chain necklaces, pendants; occasional pearls
- Chain wallets / carabiner keychains on work pants
- Canvas totes (repeatedly), small crossbody once (Jacquemus)
- Round wire-frame glasses in several pins
- Beanies (black fisherman roll)

## Grooming context (several pins are hair references)
Medium-length textured/curly hair — shag, curly fringe with taper/low fade, wavy
grown-out cuts. Mustache appears twice. This matters for "complete the look" advice.

## Brand anchors (visible or captioned)
Nike (AF1/Dunk/Jordan), Fear of God Essentials, Champion, Kapital (bone knit),
Dr. Martens, Converse, Carhartt-style workwear, Copenhagen Studios (CPH90M caption),
Harley-Davidson (vintage tee), New Balance. Thrift/vintage sourcing is clearly part
of the aesthetic — "the right piece" beats "the right brand."

## What is ABSENT (inferred no-gos, confirm via votes)
- No slim/skinny fits, no dress shirts/suiting, no monogram/logo-mania luxury,
  no athleisure-as-outfit (one track-jacket summer fit is the closest),
  no dark rigid denim, no square-toe dress shoes, no performance-gear-as-fashion.

## How the scout should use this
- Default silhouette filter: relaxed/boxy/wide. A slim version of the right item is
  the wrong item.
- Tonal-first: build around olive/tan/cream/brown/washed-grey; flag one-accent pieces
  as "statement" rather than rejecting.
- Vintage/thrift-adjacent beats box-fresh: washed, faded, distressed finishes score up.
- Shoes: retro basketball/court shapes and worn leather boots; avoid chunky-tech
  dad shoes except the Yeezy-700 tonal case.
- Weight votes above all of this — this file is the prior, votes are the data.

## Second pass: images 37-50 (2026-08-07)

The core read held. Four things it changed or added:

**1. Not every corpus image is an outfit.** Images 46 and 47 are photography with no
garment focus at all (a neon night-street film still; a rainy DUMBO/Manhattan Bridge
street shot), and 37 is a hair reference. The board mixes mood and atmosphere pins in
with fits. **A scout that treats every corpus image as a product reference will invent
signal that is not there** — roughly 1 in 8 of these pins is not about clothes.

**2. Green is a recurring accent, and it was missing from the palette.** Dark/forest
green Dunk High (39) and forest-green Chuck 70 high tops with cream foxing (45). Rust /
burnt orange also appears as a tee (40). The original "one loud accent per fit" rule is
right, but the accent set is wider than the red/maroon/UNC-blue first recorded.

**3. Wide trousers in dressier fabrics — the real "corporate-presentable" answer.**
Greige pleated creased trousers with a navy crewneck and white AF1s (48), and grey wool
wide-leg trousers with an oversized crew (44). Both still relaxed and wide. This is a
genuine gap in the first pass, which framed bottoms as denim/carpenter/cargo only. For
the `corporate-presentable` mode, the answer is clearly **pleated wide trousers, not
slim tailoring**.

**4. Footwear specifics tightened.** Chuck 70 specifically (not generic Converse), and
the cream/egret foxing is the preferred version. Jordan 1 high in Chicago red/white with
a yellowed cream midsole (43). White AF1s appear in 38, 41, 48 and 49 — by a wide margin
the single most-worn shoe on the whole board.

Smaller confirmations and one counter-signal:
- Raw, frayed and cut hems recur (40, 45, 49), including blown-out knees. Distress is
  wanted, not tolerated.
- Beanies are cream (38, 50) and grey (44) as often as black — the "black fisherman roll"
  note was too narrow.
- Cream/white jeans (50) and cream canvas totes with type on them (50) confirm the tonal
  end of the board.
- Newly visible brands: **Patagonia** (vintage tee, 40) and **Ader Error** (44).
- Crew socks are deliberately visible above cuffed hems (40).
- COUNTER-SIGNAL: the chain in 49 reads gold/yellow, not silver. Silver is still ~95% of
  the jewelry, but "silver only" is slightly too strong a rule to filter on.
